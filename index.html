About Me

I'm a data scientist and machine learning engineer, currently pursuing an M.S. in Computer Science at Columbia University. Before Columbia, I completed an M.Eng. in Systems Engineering at Cornell University, with coursework focused on AI and data science, and a B.S. in Data Science from Duke Kunshan University and Duke University.

My recent research is in embodied AI. As a Robotics Machine Learning Research Assistant at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, I co-first-authored DASH, a training-free inference acceleration framework for World Action Models that is now under review at AAAI. It reaches 2.16×–2.68× end-to-end speedups on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark with only 0.1–0.3 pp loss in success rate, and a 2.98× speedup on a real Unitree G1. Earlier, as an undergraduate, I published a first-author paper at the IEEE International Conference on Communications (ICC) 2024 under the supervision of Prof. Peng Sun and Prof. Azzedine Boukerche.

I enjoy the applied side just as much. At Columbia Business School, I work as a Data Analyst, building a PostgreSQL warehouse and a Power BI dashboard over 497K distinct job postings from 10 top employers. At Humanwell Pharmaceutical, I built an ensemble model that predicts Mean Squared Displacement from molecular structures and deployed it as a REST API, which reduced experiments and saved about $25K in three months. Outside of work, I won 1st place in the Creative Machine Lab Foosball Cup with a reinforcement learning policy, and competed in the Child Mind Institute Kaggle competition on detecting behavior from wearable sensor data.

I'm interested in Data Scientist and Machine Learning Engineer roles where I can apply this work to challenging real-world problems.

Publications

DASH: A Training-Free Drift-Adaptive Speculative Inference Framework for World Action Models

Pan Zhao*, George Wang*, Jingwu Luo, Yuhao Lei, Renda Jian, Zihan Wu, Chen Zhang, Guanghui He

Manuscript under review · * Co-first authors

A training-free framework that speeds up inference for World Action Models. Profiling showed that iterative flow-matching action generation takes 73.7–85.0% of per-query latency, and DASH cuts the average number of sequential flow-matching calls from 10 to 2.46 without retraining the model.

Key Results:

  • 2.16×–2.68× end-to-end speedups with only 0.1–0.3 pp success-rate loss
  • Evaluated on 5,000 RoboTwin 2.0 rollouts across 50 tasks and 2 World Action Models
  • 2.98× end-to-end speedup on a real Unitree G1 across 3 real-world tasks
  • Success-rate loss reduced from 23.4 pp to 6.7 pp versus naive 2-step inference, a 71% reduction in degradation

SK-SVR-CNN: A Hybrid Approach for Traffic Flow Prediction with Signature PDE Kernel and Convolutional Neural Networks

Gezhi Wang, Zepu Wang, Peng Sun, Azzedine Boukerche

IEEE International Conference on Communications (ICC), 2024

Developed a novel hybrid approach combining Signature PDE Kernel with Support Vector Regression (SVR) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for accurate traffic flow prediction. The method leverages the mathematical properties of signature kernels to capture temporal dependencies in traffic data, while CNN extracts spatial features from traffic network topology.

Key Results:

  • Improved prediction accuracy compared to traditional methods
  • Enhanced handling of complex traffic patterns and temporal dependencies
  • Robust performance across different traffic network configurations
  • Efficient computational approach for real-time traffic management
IEEE Xplore
